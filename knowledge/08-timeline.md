# 08. Timeline, 1945 to 2026

Dates are as the sources give them; where sources disagree the alternatives are noted.
Biographies are in `people/`.

## Before the commission

| When | What |
|---|---|
| July 20, 1939 | Edward Michael Scheidt born, San Bernardino County, California |
| November 14, 1945 | Herbert James Sanborn Jr. born, Washington, DC; Trinity detonated the same year |
| 1957 | Scheidt graduates Cor Jesu High School, New Orleans; joins the Army, Signals Intelligence |
| 1963 | Scheidt hired by the CIA Office of Communications; posted to Vientiane under State Department cover |
| 1966-80 | Scheidt in Damascus, Tel Aviv, Manila, Athens; field communications, mostly one-time pads |
| 1968 or 1969 | Sanborn's BA, Randolph-Macon (paleontology, fine arts, social anthropology per Wikipedia; art history and sociology per his site); Oxford summer dig |
| 1971 | Sanborn's MFA, Pratt; first breakdown; leaves New York |
| 1980-88 | Sanborn's lodestone and compass works: North by Northwest (1981), Invisible Forces (1985, 1987), Thunderhead Horizon (1985), Coriolis (1985-92), Find the Lodestone (1985), All Things Turned to Stone (1988); NEA fellowships 1982 and 1986 |
| Late 1986 | Sanborn's second trip to Egypt (named in 2025 as figuring in K4) |

## Commission and construction

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 1988 | GSA Art-in-Architecture solicitation; panel reviews 700 slides from 275 artists; Village Voice "Art of the State" attacks the project; the K4 text and the alternate now called K5 are written |
| November 1988 | Combined NEA and CIA panel chooses Sanborn; $250,000 |
| 1988-89 | Sanborn reads everything on the CIA, favourable and not; a 1988 copper proof-of-concept plate |
| 1989 | The CIA pairs Sanborn with Scheidt; four months of lessons and design at secret locations, by voice; "four different processes, two similar and two different" |
| November 9, 1989 | Berlin Wall falls; Sanborn is mid-construction and "influenced by all that going on simultaneously" |
| December 1989 | Scheidt retires (TecSec, Vienna, Virginia, in 1990); December 15: Sanborn's letter to agency employees |
| 1989-90 | The Arizona trip for the petrified tree; K4 written and enciphered on the drive back; two and a half years of jigsaw cutting with David Sheldon and others; CIA teams scan the site nightly; a trailer of granite vanishes; the scrambled plaintext scraps shown to the Office of Historical Intelligence (three people in a dark room; the one with the institutional memory asked to leave) |
| January 1990 | Washington Post: half Vigenère, half "a modern system created for the project" |
| November 3, 1990 (Nov 1 per CIA Intellipedia; Nov 5 per Webster's dated remarks and Bauer; October per NYT 1999) | Dedication. Webster: "We like to be tested." Sealed envelope(s) to Webster: "I hope you can keep a secret." Sixteen employees petition against the "meaningless" art |
| November 11, 1990 | Sanborn to the Virginia Museum of Fine Arts: "Two and a half years is long enough to work on one project, and I'm glad it's over" |
