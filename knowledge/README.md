# Knowledge base for the K4 loop

Everything the loop agent must know before proposing an experiment. Read all numbered
files in full, every session, before the first edit. They are written to be read, not
skimmed: the clue that breaks K4 is as likely to be a throwaway remark in a 2005 interview
as a statistic.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `01-facts.md` | The object: ciphertext, tableau, cribs, K1-K3 solutions and errors, Morse, site |
| `02-sanborn-said.md` | Every Sanborn statement that bears on K4, dated, verbatim where possible |
| `03-scheidt-said.md` | Every Scheidt statement that bears on K4, same treatment |
| `04-structure.md` | What the 97 letters and 24 cribs prove and suggest, with the numbers |
| `05-attacks.md` | Everything tried, by whom, with bounds, and every refuted claim |
| `06-hypotheses.md` | The live hypothesis families, evidence for and against, and open questions |
| `07-people.md` | Who is who in one paragraph each; full biographies in `people/` |
| `08-timeline.md` | 1939 to 2026, dated to the day where the sources allow |
| `09-contradictions.md` | Where the record disagrees with itself, and Sanborn's own disinformation warnings |
| `10-sanborn-the-artist.md` | The oeuvre, the obsessions, the working habits |
| `11-clue-register.md` | Every hint with its readings, how each becomes an experiment, and its test status |
| `12-assumptions.md` | What the project takes for granted: each assumption's source, firmness, what stands on it, and the cheapest check |
| `people/` | Biographies: Sanborn, Scheidt, and the solvers and community |
| `sources/` | Full text of 71 primary sources. Open the one a hypothesis rests on before you trust the quote here. |

Deeper layers, for when a family is opened or a claim needs checking:

- `../research_notes/Kryptos K4 success criteria/` holds nine long research notes with
  source links for every claim.
- `../reports/Kryptos K4 success criteria.md` is the synthesized report.

Conventions. Positions are 1-indexed; position 1 is the O of OBKR. "Verbatim" means the
words are the speaker's as printed by the cited outlet. "Via" means a reporter's or
compiler's paraphrase. "Secondhand" means a dinner or lunch recollection posted by an
attendee. Treat secondhand items as leads, not facts.
