# F15.1: what K4 says, a content model by reading

Item F15.1 (issue #118), 2026-11-04. A reading, with no run behind it. It is the input to F15.2, the drafts corpus. Sources: K1-K3's
plaintexts and the Morse (`knowledge/01-facts.md`), every statement on K4's content (`knowledge/02-sanborn-said.md`,

## (a) What K1-K3 and the Morse do

| | K0 (Morse) | K1 | K2 | K3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Form | single words, padded | one aphorism, 63 letters | a report in short sentences, with three questions | a paraphrase of Carter's diary, 336 letters |
| Theme | VIRTUALLY INVISIBLE, SHADOW FORCES, LUCID MEMORY, T IS YOUR POSITION, SOS | shading, light and illusion | something invisible, sent underground to an unknown place; buried; its exact location; a last message; coordinates | opening a sealed tomb by candle; seeing inside |
| Voice | none | third person, present tense | they and he, past tense; questions with no answers | I, past tense |
| Mechanics | one misspelling (DIGETAL) | one misspelling (IQLUSION) | X as a full stop (5 times); numbers spelled out (THIRTYEIGHTDEGREES...); one misspelling (UNDERGRUUND); `?` carved and not enciphered; ends XLAYERTWO | one misspelling (DESPARATLY); X before the last question, Q for `?` |

Read together, the four parts are one movement: something hidden, then its place, then the act of opening it and looking
in. K3 stops on an unanswered question, CAN YOU SEE ANYTHING. Carter's reply, "Yes, wonderful things", is not on the
sculpture. Every part has exactly one misspelling, and K2 and K3 both use X as a stop. K2 gives a place as a measurement,
spelled out in full. Each part's voice differs from the last, so K4 need not share K3's I.

## (b) What Sanborn has said about K4's content

- **It is his prose.** "All of the passages are fiction writings of mine" (2005); "the last thing I wrote was on that drive"
  (FT 2025); "while I was writing the plain text of Kryptos in 1988" (letter, Nov 2025). The 1991 hedge, "either a
  quotation or it's something I've written", is the only opening for a source. #117 found no published text holding
  both phrases.
- **Its subject.** "It has a lot to do with secrecy. It has a lot to do with finding something, or opening something, or
  discovering something that's secret" (1991). "Putting a negative message there was just too easy" (1991b). He wrote it
  thinking of "the work of the Agency... the things that happened back in the 1970s" (1991b).
- **Two events.** "My second trip to Egypt in late 1986, and... the fall of the Berlin Wall" (Nov 2025). "The Berlin Clock
  in K4 is the World Clock in Berlin that was the gathering place for the crowds that brought down the Berlin wall."
- **It is a message, and it points onward.** "The codes of Kryptos from the morse code at the beginning through K5 are
  about delivering a message." "Something following K4", with K2's "its buried out there somewhere" as his example. "A
  97-character phrase... in itself a riddle... going to lead to something else" (2020). "The final passage will lead in
  several directions. The word directions is, I use intentionally" (2010). "K4 has been discovered and it points in the
  direction of K5" (2025).
- **It is not fully clear.** "I'm not convinced the true meaning will be clear even then" (1991); "I would hope that it's
  never... totally clear" (1991b); "the answer will be far more ambiguous" (2005).
- **It refers back.** "I have left instructions in the earlier text that refer to later text" (2005); K4 "will come back to
  the first part" (2005).
- **What its readers say.** Kobek: "this is in English... exactly the right length. It makes semantic sense." The 1990
  copies were "sentences" cut into strips and taped out of order, made so the CIA's historical office could check the text
  was not offensive. So K4 is several sentences, with enough content that someone checked it for offence.
- **Its mechanics.** Misspellings are "deliberate" and "less important what they were... more the orientation... the
  positioning" (2003). K5 is 97 letters with BERLINCLOCK at the same places (Nov 2025).

**A dating point worth keeping.** The auction lists "an unpublished 1988 alternate K4 plaintext... now known as K5", and K5
has BERLINCLOCK at 64-74. If that 1988 text already held BERLINCLOCK, the label is older than the Wall's fall. It would
then name the World Clock as a meeting place (a usual meeting place for both Germanys: "Let's Go Europe" 1989, #117), and the crowds of
1989 were a later reading. If the 1988 text did not hold it, the 2025 statement is about the published K5. Either way, both
texts put BERLINCLOCK at 64, so the place is a fixed point of the design, not an accident of one sentence.

## (c) The layout the known letters fix

```
 1-21  (21 letters)  ?
22-34  EASTNORTHEAST
35-63  (29 letters)  ?
64-74  BERLINCLOCK
75-97  (23 letters)  ?
```

- **EASTNORTHEAST is a bearing**, a surveyor's or navigator's word (#117: wire copy does not use it). In English it follows
  a verb of motion or facing, or a distance: `...FEET`, `...PACES`, `...MILES`, `...DEGREES`, `HEADING`, `FACING`,
  `POINTING`, `LIES`, `TRAVEL`, `LOOK`. The 21 letters before it are enough for a spelled number and a unit:
  `ONEHUNDREDANDTENFEET` is 20. K2's coordinates spend 152 letters on spelled numbers, so a spelled distance here would be in
  house style.
- **BERLINCLOCK is a noun phrase**, so the letters at 58-63 are most likely an article or a preposition with one:
  `ATTHE` (59-63), `OFTHE`, `FROMTHE` (57-63), `TOTHE`, `NEARTHE`, `UNDERTHE`, or a sentence start after an X
  (`...X THE BERLINCLOCK...` puts X at 60 and THE at 61-63).
- **35-63 (29 letters)** begins straight after a bearing: `OF`, `FROM`, `TOWARD`, `X`, or a clause.
- **75-97 (23 letters)** ends the text. An `X` at 75 would be in K2's style. The end of K1-K3 leans to a question or a
  pointer onward (K2: XLAYERTWO; K3: CAN YOU SEE ANYTHING).
- No `?` can be in K4 as K2 carried it (97 letters A-Z), but K3's `Q` for `?` can be.

### Bearings checked

Is Berlin really east-northeast of anything Sanborn named? Computed: from Langley, Berlin is 44 degrees by great circle
(NE) and 78 by rhumb line (ENE is 56-79). Cairo is 56 and 96; the Valley of the Kings 58 and 98. Neither Cairo nor the
Valley of the Kings to Berlin comes near ENE (both about 335). So no clean ENE bearing links the two events or Langley to
Berlin, and a geographic bearing, if there is one, is local: on the Agency's grounds (K2's courtyard and the USGS marker
Sanborn paced from), or on the World Clock's own compass-rose mosaic (laid 1969).

## Content hypotheses

Each is a guess about what the 97 letters say. A draft (F15.2) is written under one hypothesis. The words and places
below are predictions, not checks: F15.4 tests phrases at places only where a third of the drafts agree.

**H1. A paced location, K2's way** (C23, K2's coordinates; the USGS marker Sanborn paced from, "an act I could have
carried out"). The text gives the buried thing's place as a distance and bearing from a landmark on the grounds, in
spelled numbers. Predicted: a spelled number and unit ending at 21 (`...FEET`, `...PACES`, `...YARDS`, `...METERS`);
`OFTHE`/`FROMTHE` and a landmark at 35-45 (`MARKER`, `STONE`, `TREE`, `POOL`, `COMPASS`, `ENTRANCE`, `LODESTONE`); a
second leg or a comparison to the Berlin Clock at 58-63 (`LIKETHE`, `ASTHE`). Voice: K2's, impersonal, past or
imperative.

**H2. A meeting at the World Clock: the agent's instruction** ("delivering a message"; K2's "this was his last message";
the Agency's work; the World Clock as Berlin's standard rendezvous). The text is a tradecraft message: where to go, when,
what to bring or look for. Predicted: `MEET`, `CONTACT`, `COURIER`, `SIGNAL`, `DROP`, `PACKAGE`, `MESSAGE`; a time, in
words (`ATMIDNIGHT`, `ATNOON`, `ATSIXPM`); `ATTHE` at 59-63; after 74, the next step or a recognition phrase. Voice:
imperative or second person. Fits "something following K4" (the instruction leads on) and the strip copies' sentence
form.

**H3. Berlin, November 1989: the crowds and the Wall.** A witness account, first person and past tense as in K3. Predicted:
`CROWDS`, `GATHERED`, `THOUSANDS`, `WALL`, `GATE`, `EASTBERLIN`, `NOVEMBER`, `NINETEENEIGHTYNINE`, `FREEDOM`; EASTNORTHEAST as
the direction the crowd moved or faced; `ATTHE` at 59-63 (`GATHERED AT THE BERLINCLOCK`). The weakest fit to "secrecy...
opening something" unless the Wall is the thing opened, which is close to K3: a barrier breached, people peering through.

**H4. The two events joined: a breach, seen twice.** K3 opens a sealed chamber and peers through a hole; in 1989 the Wall
is breached and crowds look through. K4 would pair them ("the tomb... the Wall"), the "relationship between all parts"
he promised. Predicted: `BREACH`, `HOLE`, `WALL`, `OPENED`, `LIGHT`, `CANDLE`, `TOMB`, `CHAMBER`, `WONDERFUL` (Carter's
missing reply), `THINGS`; EASTNORTHEAST as the direction of light or of one place from the other. Voice: K3's I.

**H5. Light and shadow: a sun bearing and a time** (K1, the Morse's SHADOW and POSITION; the Code Room's key SHADOW; the
1992 catalogue's raking light that reveals buried structures; the compass not oriented north). A shadow or a beam points
east-northeast at a time read from a clock. Predicted: `SHADOW`, `SUN`, `LIGHT`, `FALLS`, `POINTS`, `CAST`, `NOON`,
`HOUR`, `OCLOCK`, `WHEN`, `TIME`; the clock as time-teller rather than place (`TIME OF THE BERLINCLOCK`, `BYTHE`). Voice:
impersonal, present tense, as K1.

**H6. K3 continued: Carter's answer.** K4 picks up at CAN YOU SEE ANYTHING with the answer and what was found, then turns
to the second event. Predicted: `YES`, `WONDERFUL`, `THINGS`, `GOLD`, `STRANGE`, `ANIMALS`, `STATUES` in 1-21 (Carter:
"strange animals, statues and gold"); the turn to Berlin after 35. Risk: "fiction writings of mine" and the 1990 strips argue
for his prose, not more Carter; K3's sentence would leave too little room for both events.

**H7. A pointer onward: what follows K4** ("something following K4"; "lead in several directions"; K5 "more global").
The text names where the next thing is or how to read it, possibly using the sculpture's own parts as landmarks.
Predicted: `NEXT`, `FOLLOW`, `FIND`, `LOOK`, `DIRECTION(S)`, `LAYER`, `THREE`, `FIVE`, `KRYPTOS`, `PLATE`; an ending that
asks or orders (`QUESTION`, `WHERE`, `SEEK`). Overlaps H1 and H2; in F15.2 it is the mechanics (an onward instruction
at the end) more than a separate topic.

Ranking for F15.2: H2, H1, H4, H5 first (each fits both a bearing and a clock named in one breath, Sanborn's "secrecy...
finding... opening", and "delivering a message"); H3, H6, H7 second. F15.2 writes about 50 drafts for each of the seven, so
the ranking matters only if the corpus is cut.

## Mechanics to sample in F15.2

From (a): X stops (none, one, two or three, as K2's density over 97 letters), spelled numbers when a number appears, one
misspelling (0, 1, 2 or 3; K1-K3 have exactly one each, so 1 is the mode), tense and person drawn per hypothesis (above).
K3's `XCANYOUSEEANYTHINGQ` shows X before a final question and Q as its mark; allow that at the end. Proper nouns allowed
(K2 has LANGLEY and WW).

## What this model cannot do

It is a prior over content, not evidence. The 1991 statement that the meaning will never be "totally clear" and the
"riddle" of 2020 mean the real text may read oddly to any model of it. F15.3 sets the bar: drafts must find at least 2 more
of 12 held-out plants than the 24 letters alone, or the family stops there.
