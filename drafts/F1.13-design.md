# F1.13 design note (desk, 2026-10-31): split the alphabet mod 13 and mod 2

Item: F1.13, autokey on one free alphabet, 130 cells K4 fits (0256), rerun with a decider of useful power.
Issue #77. Desk only: the laptop is asleep on battery with 0267 running (#109), so nothing here is measured yet.

## The problem 0256 left

0256's tempering over the whole alphabet x (26!/12 classes, see below) ends 1.0-1.5 quadgram units *under* the
true reading on ciphertext-autokey plants (desk 2026-10-30). The plants are lost to search failure, not to a
weak score. A search that reaches the optimum more often buys power directly.

## The split

Every form is linear in the numbering x: V x(p) = x(c) - x(k), B x(p) = x(k) - x(c), R x(p) = x(c) + x(k), with
k the carved letter L back (ciphertext autokey) or the plain letter L back (plaintext autokey, a chain). Z26 is
Z13 x Z2, so each equation holds separately mod 13 and mod 2.

1. **Symmetry.** x and u.x for a unit u of Z26 (12 of them) give the same reading in every form. The space is
   26!/12, about 3.4 x 10^25.
2. **Mod 13 (stage A).** y = x mod 13 puts exactly two letters on each residue (x is a bijection onto Z26). Given
   y, every decoded place is known up to its *pair*: x(p) mod 13 names a residue, which holds two letters. The 24
   known-letter equations hold mod 13 on y alone. The space is 26!/(2^13 x 12), about 4 x 10^21.
3. **Mod 2 (stage B), exact.** Given y, one bit per pair says which of its two letters is even: 2^13 = 8,192
   choices. The bit of x(p) is x(c) - x(k) mod 2, so the bits fix every place's letter inside its pair. The
   known-letter equations mod 2 cut the 8,192 further (their mod-2 rank, already counted in the 2026-10-30
   desk note). Enumerate every surviving choice, decode, score: about 8,192 x 60 places, a few ms compiled.
   Each (y, bits) pair is one x: the split loses nothing.

So the climb runs over pairings only, with the last 13 bits solved exactly instead of climbed.

## Stage A's score

A pairing must be scored before its bits are known. Surrogate: a quadgram score over the 13 pair classes, the
English quadgram probability of a window summed over its 16 letter expansions (compiled; 16 lookups per window).
This is an upper bound on what stage B can reach, which is what a climb over pairings wants. Every K rounds, and
at the end, run stage B exactly on the current and best pairings and record the true score. Plaintext autokey:
the free chain starts become free pair residues in A and free bits in B; same treatment.

Moves in A: swap two letters on different residues (keeps two per residue); a swap that breaks a mod-13 check is
repaired by a second swap that restores it (0139's move, now in a smaller space), or dropped.

  how often the split climb's best reading scores at or above the plant's true reading, and how often it *is*

- Same on the 24 plaintext-autokey plants (0256: 4 of 24).
- Whether the surrogate's best pairings contain the true pairing's stage-B optimum: a test of the surrogate alone,
  run with the true pairing put into the starting pool.
- Cost per climb, then a cost line for the full rerun: 130 K4 cells, copies to 12 escalated to 100, 10 stand-ins,
