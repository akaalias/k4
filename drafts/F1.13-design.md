# F1.13 design note (desk, 2026-10-31): split the alphabet mod 13 and mod 2

Item: F1.13, autokey on one free alphabet, 130 cells K4 fits (0256), rerun with a decider of useful power.
Issue #77. Desk only: the laptop is asleep on battery with 0267 running (#109), so nothing here is measured yet.

## The problem 0256 left

0256's tempering over the whole alphabet x (26!/12 classes, see below) ends 1.0-1.5 quadgram units *under* the
true reading on ciphertext-autokey plants (desk 2026-10-30). The plants are lost to search failure, not to a
weak score. A search that reaches the optimum more often buys power directly.

## The split

Every form is linear in the numbering x: V x(p) = x(c) - x(k), B x(p) = x(k) - x(c), R x(p) = x(c) + x(k), with
k the carved letter L back (ciphertext autokey) or the plain letter L back (plaintext autokey, a chain). Z26 is
Z13 x Z2, so each equation holds separately mod 13 and mod 2.

1. **Symmetry.** x and u.x for a unit u of Z26 (12 of them) give the same reading in every form. The space is
   26!/12, about 3.4 x 10^25.
2. **Mod 13 (stage A).** y = x mod 13 puts exactly two letters on each residue (x is a bijection onto Z26). Given
   y, every decoded place is known up to its *pair*: x(p) mod 13 names a residue, which holds two letters. The 24
   known-letter equations hold mod 13 on y alone. The space is 26!/(2^13 x 12), about 4 x 10^21.
3. **Mod 2 (stage B), exact.** Given y, one bit per pair says which of its two letters is even: 2^13 = 8,192
   choices. The bit of x(p) is x(c) - x(k) mod 2, so the bits fix every place's letter inside its pair. The
   known-letter equations mod 2 cut the 8,192 further (their mod-2 rank, already counted in the 2026-10-30
   desk note). Enumerate every surviving choice, decode, score: about 8,192 x 60 places, a few ms compiled.
   Each (y, bits) pair is one x: the split loses nothing.

So the climb runs over pairings only, with the last 13 bits solved exactly instead of climbed.

## Stage A's score

A pairing must be scored before its bits are known. Surrogate: a quadgram score over the 13 pair classes, the
English quadgram probability of a window summed over its 16 letter expansions (compiled; 16 lookups per window).
This is an upper bound on what stage B can reach, which is what a climb over pairings wants. Every K rounds, and
at the end, run stage B exactly on the current and best pairings and record the true score. Plaintext autokey:
the free chain starts become free pair residues in A and free bits in B; same treatment.

Moves in A: swap two letters on different residues (keeps two per residue); a swap that breaks a mod-13 check is
repaired by a second swap that restores it (0139's move, now in a smaller space), or dropped.

## What to measure first (desk, before any run)

- On 0256's 24 ciphertext-autokey plant cells (12 clean, 12 Sanborn-style; their seeds are in 0256's attack.py):
  how often the split climb's best reading scores at or above the plant's true reading, and how often it *is*
  the plant. 0256: 8 of 24 found. Bar to go on: 16 of 24 (two thirds) at equal or lower cost per climb.
- Same on the 24 plaintext-autokey plants (0256: 4 of 24).
- Whether the surrogate's best pairings contain the true pairing's stage-B optimum: a test of the surrogate alone,
  run with the true pairing put into the starting pool.
- Cost per climb, then a cost line for the full rerun: 130 K4 cells, copies to 12 escalated to 100, 10 stand-ins,
  48 plants, as 0256.

If the bar is met: draft `drafts/attack-f1.13-draft.py` from 0256's attack.py with the split climb in place of
`ptemper`/`keep`, D1-style one-run decision, and its own plants in the same run. If not, write the shortfall back
to F1.13's row with the numbers, and keep it open for a different search.

## Measured on the desk, 2026-10-31 (laptop on AC, beside 0267)

Probes: `drafts/f1.13-split-probe.py` (the split climb), `drafts/f1.13-ceiling-probe.py` (is the true reading the best
the cell allows?), `drafts/f1.13-keep-probe.py` (0256's check-keeping climb past lag 12). All regenerate 0256's 48
plants from its seeds and code; their cells and 0256's scores match 0256's record.

**1. The split climb does not meet the bar.** Stage A on the surrogate (16 replicas x 300 rounds x 1,000 moves, about
3-7 s a plant), first 6 ciphertext-autokey plants: reached the true score 3 of 6, read 0 of 6. The three "reached"
are at lags 69-73, where a wrong reading beats the true one (see 2); at lags 46-64 the climb ended under the true
reading as 0256 did. The surrogate itself misleads: at lag 52 (ct/5) its best pairing scores -3.665 against the true
pairing's -3.809, so even a perfect stage-A search would not land on the plant. Scoring each pairing exactly instead
(GF(2) elimination of the parity checks, every consistent parity choice decoded; 30 rounds) was no better (lags 46,
64: best -6.12 and -4.78 against true -4.30 and -4.08) and slow at long lags, where few checks leave up to 2^13
choices. Not taken further.

**2. Ceiling: in many of these cells no search can find the plant by this score.** Started from the plant's own
alphabet (0256's tempering, one replica, low temperature, 100,000 moves), the climb leaves the true reading for a
better-scoring wrong one in 5 of 24 ct plants (lags 71-73, 24-26 places) and 5 of 24 pt plants (lags 32-52). And
0256's own climbs, from random starts, already scored *above* the true reading in 10 of the 13 ct plants at lags
66-73 and in 10 of the 11 pt plants at lags 32-52: there the plant is out-scored by noise, not missed by the search.
Where the true reading is the best found (ct lags 41-56, pt lags 14-24), 0256's climbs end 0.25-2.2 units below it (median about 1.1):
search failure.

| Cells (K4's count) | Lost by | What would help |
|---|---|---|
| ct lags 64-73 (20), pt lags 31-52 (47) | the score: wrong readings beat the true one with 24-33 places (ct) or 30+ free chain starts (pt) | nothing short of a stronger language model or more text; sampled at 0256's power, which is the ceiling |
| ct lags 32-61 (23), pt lags 8-30 (40) | the search: the true reading is the best found but no climb reaches it | a new search |

**3. 0256's check-keeping climb past lag 12** (pt plants at lags 14-24, from the witness, 16 x 300 x 1,000, 4-9 min a
plant on the shared laptop): 0 of 8 reach the true reading. Lags 14, 14, 15, 17, 18, 21, 23, 24: keep -6.91, -7.06,
-5.66, -5.50, -5.44, -5.76, -5.17, -4.98 against true -4.18, -4.27, -4.29, -4.15, -4.35, -4.36, -4.54, -4.39 (0256's
tempering -6.28, -6.42, -6.03, -5.52, -5.56, -5.42, -5.20, -5.10); 25-35 of 97 letters right. No better than 0256.

**4. A constructive beam along the chains** (`drafts/f1.13-beam-probe.py`, plaintext autokey). The known places are two
runs, so a step of L from a run decodes a whole run; the beam decodes place by place (places that close a check first,
then contiguity), assigning alphabet values only when a step needs one, with every relation between decoded places L
apart enforced (verify or force) after each step; partial readings scored by English letter probabilities given the
letters to the left. Checked: seeded with the plant's true alphabet it returns the plant exactly (97 of 97), and with
the true values of 13 letters it still finds it. Unseeded on pt/16 (lag 18): beams of 2,000, 20,000 and 200,000 states
all die (every state killed by a check by about the 23rd place), and with the true values of 3 letters seeded a beam of
20,000 ends at -5.56, 0256's own score. The early steps choose values that no partial English score can tell apart
(thousands of ties), so the true path is dropped before the checks bite. Not taken further.

Verdict for now: the bar (16 of 24 per kind) is not met by any of four searches (the split climb, the exact-pairing
climb, check-keeping tempering past lag 12, the chain beam). About 67 of K4's 130 cells
lose their plants to the score itself, so even a perfect search would find at most about half the plants drawn from
K4's cells. F1.13 stays open for the 63 cells where search is the loss (ct lags 32-61, pt lags 8-30). It needs a search that
neither swaps letters of the whole alphabet nor builds it from English scores of short pieces. One not tried: a beam
that branches on alphabet values only where a check is about to close, so that every kept state is already cut by the
known letters.
