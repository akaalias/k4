# 10. Sanborn the artist, as an encoder

The point of this file is a model of the man who sat in a truck in 1989 and enciphered
97 letters by hand. Every hypothesis should pass the test "would this person have done
that, that way?"

## Formation

Only child of two Library of Congress employees: a printmaker father who ran exhibitions
for thirty years, a concert-pianist mother who was a photo researcher. Grew up in
"institutional green hallways," handled Dead Sea Scrolls as a child, researched his public
texts at the Library. Wanted to be an archaeologist; an Oxford summer dig where he found a
skeleton and a forgotten crypt passage. "My original training was in archaeology." Found
ruins by flying over them at dawn and dusk when raking light threw shadows. Pratt MFA
1971; abandoned the human form; first breakdown before graduating; anxiety and depression
since. Paranoid by his own account.

## Obsessions, by decade

1980s: invisible natural forces. Lodestones from a Utah lightning field, arrays of
hundreds of compass needles, the Coriolis force, thunderheads. The compass rose and
lodestone at the CIA entrance come from here.

1990s: invisible human forces. Kryptos, then pulped CIA documents, KGB memos enciphered
on copper and bronze (the Cyrillic Projector, Antipodes, the Code Room whose key was the
Russian for shadow), light projected through cut letters so viewers are "tainted" by the
text. Then landscape projections of perfect geometry onto buttes, surviving only as
photographs.

2000s: the moment science becomes technology. Rebuilt the Los Alamos assembly room and a
working million-volt accelerator; fissioned uranium in his studio.

2010s: forged Khmer antiquities presented as a mock auction with invented provenance.
"An ontological trouble-maker." "I do make stuff up."

2020s: Euclid and Descartes in bronze lattices. Reading prehistory.

## Working habits that bear on K4

- He builds a scientist's instrument and then personalizes it until the output is his
  alone. "It is a solid system, and I fucked with it."
- He thinks spatially and in light and shadow: "encoding systems which use pattern, light
  and shadow"; "spatial systems of encoding and decoding"; chiaroscuro as method; the
  tableau cut so it reads only from behind; raking light over ruins.
- He thinks in strata and layers: palimpsest, "layer two," pages of a document, onion,
  fossil, "layers within layers." Sections hark back: "when the fourth part is deciphered,
  it will come back to the first part."
- He leaves a signature anomaly per section: one misspelling each in K1, K2, K3, DIGETAL
  in the Morse, raised YAR, the extra L. He says the "positioning" of errors matters more
  than what they are.
- He uses X as a stop and literal question marks, writes telegraphically, asks questions,
  and ends on an unanswered one.
- His keys are words from the puzzle's own world: KRYPTOS (the title), PALIMPSEST
  (overwritten text), ABSCISSA (a coordinate), TEHb (shadow) on the Code Room. He chose
  "carefully-chosen wording."
- He releases "hidden information at a rate commensurate with its importance, and at the
  time of my choosing." Clues come on anniversaries and in pairs with misdirection
  ("several really interesting clocks in Berlin").
- He is not a mathematician and says so often; he was tutored every summer "to be able to
  get a D." Systems that "didn't necessarily depend on mathematics" were "one of my
  prerequisites."
- He composed all four texts in 1988 as fiction with an archaeological and Cold War
  vocabulary (invisibility, burial, magnetism, coordinates, a sealed chamber, Carter's
  candle), and K4 draws on Egypt 1986 and Berlin 1989. He wrote the last text on a long
  quiet drive and enciphered it the same way, with charts.
- He thinks of the whole site as one work: Morse at the entrance, the screen, the pools,
  the compass, the strata that "were supposed to parallel something in the courtyard," a
  survey marker now gone. "Having the letters sitting there in plain sight is a pretty big
  clue."
- He values the condition of being a secret above the content of the secret. "Secrecy is
  power." "Once an artwork loses its mystery, it's lost a lot." He wanted K4 to outlive
  him and then sold it when he could no longer carry it.

## What this predicts

A K4 system that a careful amateur with a chart could execute on a truck seat; that
reuses the tableau at some stage; that involves rows, columns and a reordering he would
call a matrix; that keys on a word from the sculpture's world; that hides one more
misspelling; that was altered from Scheidt's plan in a way an artist would think of and a
cryptographer would not; and whose plaintext points back at the first part and forward to
K5.
