# 09. Contradictions and disinformation

Sanborn: "I seem to like to manipulate the information coming out, in a sort of
disinformation fashion... a lot of the clues that I might give work in a sort of
disinformational way" (2005). Also: "It's not my intent to put out disinformation. I'm a
benevolent cryptographer" (2009). Both are on the record. Weigh accordingly.

## Who knows what

- Sanborn 1999: the complete answer was handed to Webster. Sanborn 2005: "I definitely
  didn't give him the last section." AP 1991 and Bauer: two envelopes, keywords and
  plaintext. Sanborn 2025: "I'm not exactly sure what I handed him, frankly."
- Scheidt 2003 (secondhand): he "did the actual encryption and design." Sanborn 2003:
  Scheidt taught, Sanborn encrypted; "compartmentalized." Sanborn 2005: Scheidt "doesn't
  know the solution." Scheidt 2005: "I know what the message was to be." Scheidt 2015: "I
  don't know the answer... I never went back to check." RR Auction 2025: the method is
  "known only to Jim Sanborn and cryptographer Ed Scheidt." Reconcilable only if Scheidt
  designed the method and knew the intended plaintext but never saw the carved K4.
- Sanborn 2005: "Do you remember what the solution is?" "No." Sanborn 2010 onward: vets
  submissions by letter position. FT 2025: "He can't remember the solved text off the top
  of his head, though he knows enough to vet submissions"; his wife "has heard the K4
  solution" but "doesn't remember it."

## The method

- Scheidt: "masking technique," 2005 and 2015. Sanborn 2019: "confused when we mentioned
  masking technique."
- Sanborn 2005: "I did use some matrix codes Ed gave me"; "double matrixes and triple
  matrixes... then went back to this table." Sanborn 2009 (oral history): matrix codes
  "are the parts of Kryptos that have already been cracked. So I can discuss them. But he
  told me about coding systems that I could then modify in a myriad of ways." Schridde
  reads this as K4 using none of the K1-K3 methods. The 2005 CNN passage was said while
  pointing at the tableau and describing the piece as a whole; it is not explicitly about
  K4. Hold both readings.
- Scheidt 2011: "not mathematical." Sanborn 2005 dinner, told Kryptos "didn't use
  mathematical matrices": "but it does." Sanborn 2025: "Who says it is even a math
  solution?"
- "One-to-one": affirmed at dinners (2011, 2013), walked back in 2019 to "97 characters
  and BERLIN at 64."
- Sanborn 2006: K1-K3 contain clues to K4; "getting those first three sections correct
  is crucial." Sanborn 2019: not necessary to know K1-K3 to solve K4. Sanborn 2013: "Yes,
  but K5 cannot be solved without K4."

## The count and the clock

- 97 (Sanborn, consistently, when pressed) against "whether it's 97, whether it's 100"
  (2005) and Scheidt's "98" (2005). The "?" is "a question mark" (2005).
- 2014: "You'd better delve into that particular clock," the Mengenlehreuhr. 2025:
  BERLINCLOCK is the World Clock on Alexanderplatz. Eleven years of base-5 work followed
  the first statement.
- 2020: NORTHEAST is "the third and to be sure final clue." EAST followed within months,
  and four thematic hints in 2025.

## Expected lifetime

- Scheidt: five years (2000), under seven (2009), "five, seven or maybe ten" (2021),
  difficulty nine out of ten. Sanborn: "weeks, months, years, or never" (1991); "I assumed
  the first three sections would be deciphered in a matter of weeks" (2009); wanted it to
  "outlive him" (2020). Bean: "the timeline is absolutely defying the Scheidt expressed
  intention."

## The errors

- Misspellings "deliberate" (Sanborn, repeatedly) against the PALIMPCEST worksheet and
  the UNDERGROUND-to-UNDERGRUUND transcription change; "Saying I was sloppy is a good way
  of saying it... if you want to say it's intentional, it's intentional" (2005). "You
  could not make any mistake with 1,800 letters" (2025) is about cutting copper, not the
  charts.
- The K2 omission: "for aesthetic reasons," refusing to call it an oversight (NSA's
  reading of his 2006 statement).

## After K4

- 1999: a riddle requiring presence at the site; 2005: "an act... and a location that's on
  the grounds"; 2010: "the final passage will lead in several directions"; 2020: "lead to
  something else"; 2025: K5, a sibling text written in 1988, "visible in a public space,"
  with "a more global reach." Wikipedia's lead over-reads this as "a fifth coded message
  will reveal itself." One editor: the letter "is vague enough where it's unclear whether
  the riddle is K4 or Kryptos as a whole."

## The fate of the secret

- "The secret will probably pass away" and "actually I have [left it in my will]" in the
  same 2005 interview; "I'd rather die knowing it wasn't cracked" (2009); posthumous auction
  (2020); lifetime sale (2025); then the words leaked anyway.

## What the record never contradicts

That K4 is 97 letters of English. That BERLINCLOCK is at 64-74 and EASTNORTHEAST at
22-34. That Sanborn wrote the text. (That he did the encryption himself is Sanborn's account, contested only by Scheidt 2003, secondhand; taken as true, A15.) That Scheidt masked
the English and never checked the result. That the sculpture's two alphabets, KRYPTOS and
standard, matter. That a solution needs the method.
